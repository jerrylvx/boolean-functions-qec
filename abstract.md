# Building quantum codes with transversal CCZ from projective geometry

Bohan Lu — Virginia Tech Applied Algebra Seminar, October 22, 2026

Quantum error-correcting codes must protect information while allowing logical operations. This talk explains how classical coding theory and finite geometry can help meet both requirements. Starting with an eight-qubit cube example, I will describe CSS encodings through classical cosets and show how weights and overlaps determine the action of single-qubit phase gates. I will distinguish the triorthogonal matrices introduced by Bravyi and Haah from the unital triorthogonal spaces classified by Nezami and Haah, and connect their indicator-polynomial method to the CCZ lower bound. A projective, 8-divisible stabilizer code then supplies a 48-point support with Z-distance at least three. A SAT search selects compatible logical operators, followed by a physical-sign test, giving a [[48,3,3]] code whose logical CCZ is implemented by 26 physical T and 22 T-dagger gates. I will sketch our independent exclusion of lengths through 38 and explain how the concurrent classification of Wills, Jain, and Singh closes lengths 39 through 46 when a diagonal Clifford correction is allowed. The talk concludes with questions about geometric completions, higher distance, and practical implementation. Joint work with Kenneth R. Brown.
