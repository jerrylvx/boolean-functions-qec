\begin{frame}[label=cube-css]{The cube code $\qcode{8}{3}{2}$}
\talktext
\begin{columns}[c,onlytextwidth]
\column{.33\textwidth}\centering
\resizebox{.95\linewidth}{!}{\cubeobject{%
\only<2>{\cubevalues{mod(\i+\j+\k,2)}}%
\only<3>{\cubeedge{000}{100}\cubering{000}\cubering{100}%
\node[left=4.5mm,text=Gold!80!black,font=\bfseries] at (v000) {$Z$};\node[right=4.5mm,text=Gold!80!black,font=\bfseries] at (v100) {$Z$};}}}
\par\scriptsize\rule{0pt}{3ex}\only<2>{Values of $u_1\oplus u_2\oplus u_3$.}\only<3>{The error $Z_{000}Z_{100}$.}
\column{.64\textwidth}
Qubits are the points $u\in\F_2^3$. A binary word is a function $c\colon\F_2^3\to\F_2$, with $c\wedge c'=cc'$ and $\wt(c)=|c^{-1}(1)|$. $\RM(r,m)$ is the evaluation code of polynomials of degree $\le r$ on $\F_2^m$.
\par\smallskip
A CSS code [CS96, Ste96] is given by $\C_2=\operatorname{rowspan}S\subset\C_1=\operatorname{rowspan}\left[\begin{smallmatrix}K\\S\end{smallmatrix}\right]$, with $\ket{x}_L\propto\sum_{v\in\C_2}\ket{xK\oplus v}$ for $x\in\F_2^k$, where $\ket c$ is the basis state labelled by $c$. $X$ checks span $\C_2$, $Z$ checks span $\C_1^\perp$, and $Z_u\ket c=(-1)^{c(u)}\ket c$.
\par
\onslide<2->{Cube: $S=\mathbf 1$, $K=(u_1;u_2;u_3)$, so $\C_1=\RM(1,3)$ and $\ket{x}_L=\bigl(\ket{x\cdot u}+\ket{1\oplus x\cdot u}\bigr)/\sqrt2$.}
\par\smallskip
\onslide<3->{$Z_{000}Z_{100}$ commutes with $X^{\otimes8}$ and acts as $(-1)^{x_1}$, so $d=2$.}
\end{columns}
\source{\refCS\par\refSt}
\note{Notation for the whole talk is on this slide. Words are functions on the cube. The CSS state is a uniform superposition over a coset of C2. Second step: the word for x equal to 111 is the parity function. Third step: the two Z errors see c(000) plus c(100), which equals x1 for every word in the coset, so they flip the sign of logical states with x1 equal to 1 and are undetected. Distance two.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}[label=cube-gate]{A transversal $T/T^\dagger$ layer gives logical CCZ on the cube}
\talktext
\begin{columns}[c,onlytextwidth]
\column{.38\textwidth}\centering
\resizebox{.92\linewidth}{!}{\cubeobject{\only<2->{\cubegates}}}
\par\scriptsize\rule{0pt}{3ex}\only<2->{$T^\dagger$ where $p(u)=u_1\oplus u_2\oplus u_3=1$ [CH17].}
\column{.59\textwidth}
$T=\operatorname{diag}(1,e^{i\pi/4})$ and $\mathrm{CCZ}\ket{x}_L=(-1)^{x_1x_2x_3}\ket{x}_L$. Both are non-Clifford, and Clifford gates alone are not universal.
A \emph{transversal} layer is a tensor product of one-qubit gates, so one gate fault affects one qubit.
\par\smallskip
\onslide<2->{With $\Gamma_u=(-1)^{p(u)}$, the layer multiplies $\ket c$ by $e^{i\pi W_\Gamma(c)/4}$, where
\[
W_\Gamma(c)=\sum_u\Gamma_u\,c(u)=d_H(p,c)-4.
\]}%
\onslide<3->{For $x\ne111$, $p\oplus c$ is nonconstant affine, so $W_\Gamma(c)=0$.
For $x=111$, $c\in\{p,1\oplus p\}$ and $W_\Gamma(c)=\mp4$, phase $-1$.}
\end{columns}
\source{\refCH}
\note{The cube code with this sign pattern is the Campbell-Howard eight-qubit example. W is an integer sum. Each one of c at a T site adds one, each one at a T-dagger site subtracts one, and W equals wt(c) minus 2 wt(p and c), which is the distance to p minus four. Nonconstant affine functions have weight four. Both words of each logical pair receive the same phase, which is what a logical gate requires.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}[label=cube-overlap]{Weights of sums expand into overlaps}
\talktext
\[
\wt(a_1\oplus\cdots\oplus a_m)=\sum_{\emptyset\ne J\subseteq\{1,\ldots,m\}}(-2)^{|J|-1}\,\wt\Bigl(\bigwedge_{j\in J}a_j\Bigr).
\]
\begin{columns}[c,onlytextwidth]
\column{.34\textwidth}\centering
\resizebox{!}{3.2cm}{\cubeobject{\cubegates\only<2>{\cubering{111}}\only<3>{\cubeedge{110}{111}\cubering{110}\cubering{111}}}}
\column{.63\textwidth}
The identity holds at each coordinate, so it also holds for $W_\Gamma$ in place of $\wt$.
Modulo 8, only the terms with $|J|\le3$ remain.
\par\medskip
\onslide<2->{$K_1\wedge K_2\wedge K_3=u_1u_2u_3$ is the vertex $111$: weight 1.}
\par\smallskip
\onslide<3->{$K_1\wedge K_2=u_1u_2$ is an edge with one $T$ and one $T^\dagger$: $W_\Gamma=0$.}
\end{columns}
\note{Check the identity at a coordinate where k of the words are one: the sum of binomial k choose t times minus two to the t minus one is one for odd k and zero for even k. Coefficients are one, minus two, four, minus eight. The cube steps show the triple overlap and a pair overlap of logical rows. These overlaps reappear in the gate condition.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}[label=cube-condition]{The gate condition is a congruence on generator rows}
\talktext
A layer $\Gamma\in\{\pm1\}^n$ implements logical CCZ if and only if
\[
W_\Gamma(xK\oplus yS)\equiv4x_1x_2x_3\pmod8\qquad\text{for all }x\in\F_2^3,\ y\in\F_2^s.
\]
The phase depends on $W_\Gamma$ mod 8. Independence of $y$ gives every word of a coset the same phase.
\onslide<2->{Expand with the overlap identity and compare coefficients of the monomials in $(x,y)$. For distinct rows $g_a,g_b,g_c$ of $G=[K;S]$:
\[
\begin{aligned}
W_\Gamma(g_a)&\equiv0\pmod8,\\
W_\Gamma(g_a\wedge g_b)&\equiv0\pmod4,\\
W_\Gamma(g_a\wedge g_b\wedge g_c)&\equiv[\{g_a,g_b,g_c\}=\{K_1,K_2,K_3\}]\pmod2.
\end{aligned}
\]}%
\onslide<3->{The cube satisfies all three: rows and faces have $W_\Gamma=0$, edges have $W_\Gamma=0$, and $u_1u_2u_3$ has weight 1.}
\note{The bracket is one when the three rows are the three logical rows and zero otherwise. Minus two times a pair overlap must vanish mod eight, which gives mod four. Four times a triple overlap must equal four times the CCZ indicator, which gives a parity. Terms of order four and higher carry a factor eight.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}[label=history]{From magic-state distillation to triorthogonal codes}
\talktext
\begin{description}[2022]\setlength{\itemsep}{0pt}
\item[2005] Bravyi--Kitaev [BK05]: magic-state distillation with ideal Clifford gates.
\item[2012] Bravyi--Haah [BH12]: triorthogonal matrices, transversal $T$ up to Clifford corrections.
\item[2017] Campbell--Howard [CH17]: distillation with multiqubit gate synthesis, including CCZ.
\item[2022] Nezami--Haah [NH22]: classify triorthogonal codes with $n+k\le38$ via $\RM$ indicators.
\item[2024] Camps-Moreno, L\'opez, Matthews et al.\ [CLM+24]: CSS-T codes from cyclic and extended cyclic codes. In 2026 [CLM+26]: all diagonal transversal gates of a CSS code.
\end{description}
\source{\refBK\par\refBH\par\refCH\par\refNH\par\refCLMa\par\refCLMb}
\note{Bravyi-Kitaev motivates distilling a non-Clifford resource with trusted Clifford operations. Bravyi-Haah turn the gate action into parity conditions on binary rows. Campbell-Howard give the CCZ version, which is used here. Nezami-Haah classify through unital spaces and Reed-Muller indicators, and their n plus k bound is not our n at most 38 result. The CSS-T line from Camps-Moreno, Lopez, Matthews and coauthors studies pairs of binary codes on which transversal T acts as a logical gate, with constructions from Reed-Muller, cyclic and extended cyclic codes. Their 2026 paper computes the diagonal transversal gates of a CSS code and recovers CSS-T, triorthogonal and divisible cases.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}[label=triortho]{A triorthogonal matrix and a triorthogonal space differ}
\talktext
\begin{columns}[T,onlytextwidth]
\column{.48\textwidth}
\textbf{Matrix $G$ [BH12]}
\[
\begin{aligned}
\wt(g_a\wedge g_b)&\equiv0\pmod2,\\
\wt(g_a\wedge g_b\wedge g_c)&\equiv0\pmod2.
\end{aligned}
\]
Indices are distinct. Odd-weight rows label logical $T$ outputs. Even-weight rows give $X$ checks.
\column{.48\textwidth}
\textbf{Space $\mathcal H$ [NH22]}
\[
\sum_{j=1}^{L}u_jv_jw_j=0\pmod2
\]
for all $u,v,w\in\mathcal H\subseteq\F_2^L$, including repeated vectors.
\par\smallskip
\emph{Unital}: $\mathbf1_L\in\mathcal H$.
Every word in $\mathcal H$ has even weight.
\end{columns}
\par\medskip
Nezami--Haah classify parent spaces, then obtain codes by puncturing and deleting coordinates.
\takeaway{CCZ needs an odd logical triple. Triorthogonal matrices have even distinct-row triples.}
\source{\refBH\par\refNH}
\note{Odd rows violate the space definition when repeated three times, so the row span of a triorthogonal matrix is not a triorthogonal space. Setting u equal to v equal to w proves even weight. Puncturing a parent space produces odd logical rows in descendant T codes. The CCZ condition has K1 and K2 and K3 with an odd triple overlap, so it needs generalized conditions.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}[label=ch]{The nine CH conditions}
\talktext
\begin{columns}[c,onlytextwidth]
\column{.42\textwidth}\centering
{\footnotesize\begin{tabular}{ll}\toprule
K1 & $\wt(K_i)\equiv0$\\ K2 & $\wt(K_i\wedge K_j)\equiv0$\\K3 & $\wt(K_1\wedge K_2\wedge K_3)\equiv1$\\\midrule
KS1 & $\wt(K_i\wedge S_l)\equiv0$\\KS2 & $\wt(K_i\wedge K_j\wedge S_l)\equiv0$\\KS3 & $\wt(K_i\wedge S_l\wedge S_m)\equiv0$\\\midrule
S1 & $\wt(S_l)\equiv0$\\S2 & $\wt(S_l\wedge S_m)\equiv0$\\S3 & $\wt(S_l\wedge S_m\wedge S_r)\equiv0$\\\bottomrule\end{tabular}}
\par\scriptsize All mod 2, indices distinct.
\column{.55\textwidth}
Reduce the three row congruences mod 2. Since $\Gamma_j$ is odd, $W_\Gamma\equiv\wt\pmod 2$, and the result is these nine parities [CH17].
\par\medskip
They give CCZ up to a diagonal Clifford correction: \emph{quasi-transversal} CCZ.
The cube satisfies all nine.
\par\medskip
\onslide<2->{A physical $T/T^\dagger$ layer with no correction needs the signed congruences mod 8 and mod 4. These are conditions on $\Gamma$ as well as on $G$.}
\end{columns}
\takeawayat{2-}{The CH conditions are necessary. A code can satisfy all nine and admit no sign vector $\Gamma$.}
\source{\refCH}
\note{K1 to K3 involve logical rows only, KS1 to KS3 are mixed, S1 to S3 are stabilizer only. K3 is the single odd condition and selects CCZ. The 47-qubit comparison later is a code that satisfies all nine and has no sign vector.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}[label=results]{Result: a 48-qubit code with a transversal $T/T^\dagger$ CCZ}
\talktext
\begin{block}{The construction [LB26]}
$Q_{48}=\qcode{48}{3}{3}$, with $(d_X,d_Z)=(16,3)$. A layer of $26\,T+22\,T^\dagger$ implements logical CCZ with no Clifford correction.
\end{block}
\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{3pt}
\item The stabilizer code is fixed by projective geometry: $\PG(5,2)\setminus\PG(3,2)$.
\item The logical rows come from a SAT search, and the signs from a linear system over $\mathbb Z_4$.
\item Lower bound: no code with $k=3$, $d_Z\ge3$, and the nine CH conditions has $n\le38$. Lengths 39 to 46 are excluded by [WJS26].
\item The same construction gives codes at 13 block lengths from 48 to 496.
\end{itemize}
\takeaway{The code satisfies the error-correction constraints and the gate constraints at once.}
\source{\refLB\par\refWJS}
\note{State the result now that every term has been defined on the cube. Distance three corrects one error. The transversal layer keeps a single gate fault on a single qubit. The remainder of the talk explains the three steps and then the bounds. The 39 to 46 exclusion is from Wills, Jain and Singh, not from our paper.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}[label=fix-s]{Fix $S$ using finite/projective geometry code}
\talktext
\begin{enumerate}\setlength{\itemsep}{6pt}
\item<1-> Columns of $S$ distinct and nonzero: $d_Z\ge3$.
\item<2-> Every word of $\C_2$ has weight $\equiv0\pmod8$. By the overlap identity, pair overlaps are $\equiv0\pmod4$ and triple overlaps are even, so S1--S3 hold.
\item<3-> Search the logical rows $K$ with SAT for K1--K3 and KS1--KS3.
\item<4-> Solve for the signs $\Gamma$ over $\mathbb Z_4$.
\end{enumerate}
\takeawayat{3-}{The SAT search has no distance constraint and does not search over $S$.}
\note{Eight-divisibility is a design choice for the stabilizer code, not part of the definition of the CH conditions. Steps one and two are classical coding theory. Step three is the only search. Step four is a linear-algebra test.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}[label=projective]{Projectivity rules out weight-one and weight-two $Z$ logicals}
\talktext
Let $\beta_j$ be column $j$ of $S$. A $Z$ error at site $j$ has syndrome $\beta_j$.
\[
\beta_j\ne0\ \Longrightarrow\ \text{weight-one errors are detected},\qquad
\beta_i\ne\beta_j\ \Longrightarrow\ \beta_i\oplus\beta_j\ne0.
\]
Distinct nonzero columns are points of $\PG(s-1,2)$, the $2^s-1$ nonzero vectors of $\F_2^s$.
\takeaway{$d_Z\ge3$ follows from the stabilizer columns alone, for any logical rows.}
\note{This is the parity-check argument for a distance-three classical code. S is the X check matrix, so it detects Z errors. The statement covers d_Z only. d_X is computed for the final code.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}[label=support]{The support is projective space minus a subspace}
\talktext
\[
\mathcal P=\PG(5,2)\setminus\PG(3,2),\qquad |\mathcal P|=63-15=48.
\]
Write $\beta=(u,w)\in\F_2^4\times\F_2^2$. The removed $\PG(3,2)$ is the set of 15 nonzero points with $w=00$.
\begin{center}\resizebox{!}{25mm}{\supportobject{1}{\only<2->{\fill[white,opacity=.8] (-.45,.45) rectangle (3.45,-3.45);\node[text=Gold!80!black,font=\small\bfseries] at (1.5,-1.5) {removed};}}}\end{center}
\onslide<2->{The 6 coordinate functions $u_1,\ldots,u_4,w_1,w_2$ are the rows of $S$.}
\takeawayat{2-}{Three blocks of 16 points give a projective $[48,6]$ stabilizer code.}
\note{First step: all 63 points of PG(5,2), grouped by w. Second step: remove the 15 points with w equal to 00. Each remaining block is a coset of F2 to the fourth. Inside each block the rows are u1 u2 and the columns are u3 u4, both in Gray order. This picture is used for the rest of the construction.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}[label=weights]{Hyperplane sections give the two nonzero weights}
\talktext
A word of $\C_2$ evaluates $\ell(u,w)=\lambda\cdot u\oplus\mu\cdot w$ on $\mathcal P$, and $\wt(\ell)=|\mathcal P|-|\mathcal P\cap\{\ell=0\}|$.
\begin{center}\resizebox{!}{20mm}{\supportobject{0}{\only<1>{\supportmark{\ua}{fill=Gold}}\only<2>{\supportmark{\wa}{fill=Gold}}}}\end{center}
\only<1>{$\lambda\ne0$, for example $\ell=u_1$: eight points in each block, weight $3\cdot8=24$.}%
\only<2->{$\lambda=0$, for example $\ell=w_1$: two full blocks, weight $2\cdot16=32$.}
\par\smallskip
Projective two-weight codes correspond to finite-geometry sets with two hyperplane intersection sizes [CK86].
\takeawayat{2-}{Weights 24 and 32 are multiples of 8, so S1--S3 hold.}
\source{\refCK}
\note{Gold squares mark the support of the stabilizer word. A nonzero lambda cuts each block in half. Lambda zero and mu nonzero selects two of the three blocks. Every nonzero word has positive weight, so the six rows are independent. Uniqueness of this support is in the paper and is not needed here.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}[label=indicator]{Column indicators record the generator matrix}
\talktext
Let $M$ have distinct columns in $\F_2^r$. Its \emph{column indicator} [NH22] is the Boolean function
\[
\chi_M(v)=1\iff v\text{ is a column of }M.
\]
Write $\chi_M$ as a multilinear polynomial. Then $\chi_M\in\RM(\deg\chi_M,r)$ and $\wt(\chi_M)$ is the number of columns.
\par\medskip
\onslide<2->{Cube: the columns of $G=[K;S]$ are $(u,1)$ for $u\in\F_2^3$. In coordinates $(x,y)\in\F_2^3\times\F_2$,
\[
\chi_G(x,y)=y.
\]}%
\onslide<3->{$Q_{48}$: the columns of $S$ are the points with $w\ne00$, so
\[
\chi_S(u,w)=1+(1+w_1)(1+w_2)=w_1+w_2+w_1w_2.
\]}%
\source{\refNH}
\note{The indicator representation follows Nezami and Haah. Coordinates of F2 to the r index the rows of M, so a column is a point. The cube indicator has degree one. The 48-qubit stabilizer indicator has degree two and does not depend on u.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}[label=inner]{The CH conditions are inner products with monomials}
\talktext
For Boolean functions on $\F_2^r$, let $\langle f,g\rangle=\sum_{v}f(v)g(v)\bmod2$ [CH17].
Index the rows of $G$ by coordinates $z=(x,y)$. For a set $T$ of rows, $m_T=\prod_{t\in T}z_t$ and
\[
\langle\chi_G,m_T\rangle=\wt\Bigl(\bigwedge_{t\in T}g_t\Bigr)\bmod2.
\]
\onslide<2->{The nine CH conditions say: $\langle\chi_G,x_1x_2x_3\rangle=1$, and $\langle\chi_G,m\rangle=0$ for every other monomial of degree 1 to 3.}
\par\medskip
\onslide<3->{Since $\RM(3,r)^\perp=\RM(r-4,r)$ and $y_1\cdots y_s$ is one solution [LB26],
\[
\chi_G\in y_1\cdots y_s+\varepsilon\delta_0+\RM(r-4,r),\qquad r=3+s,\ \varepsilon=n\bmod2.
\]
The cube indicator $\chi_G=y_1$ is this representative with $s=1$.}
\source{\refCH\par\refLB}
\note{An overlap of rows is a monomial in the row coordinates, evaluated on the columns. Summing over columns is summing chi_G times the monomial over all points. The function y1 to ys is one at the eight points x comma all-ones, so its inner product with a monomial in x of degree d is two to the three minus d mod two, which is one only for x1x2x3. delta zero is the indicator of the origin and fixes the parity of the weight. The coset statement is a lemma in our paper.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}[label=labels]{The label map turns logical rows into Boolean unknowns}
\talktext
\begin{columns}[c,onlytextwidth]
\column{.55\textwidth}
Column $\beta=(u,w)\in\mathcal P$ of $S$ carries a label $\kappa(\beta)\in\F_2^3$, the column of $K$ above it [LB26]:
\[
\chi_G(x,u,w)=\chi_S(u,w)\prod_{i=1}^{3}\bigl(1+x_i+\kappa_i(u,w)\bigr).
\]
The CH conditions become
\[
\bigoplus_{\beta\in\mathcal P}m_{T_K}\bigl(\kappa(\beta)\bigr)\,m_{T_S}(\beta)=[T=\{K_1,K_2,K_3\}].
\]
S1--S3 have $T_K=\emptyset$ and hold by geometry. The rest have degree at most 3 in the label bits.
\column{.42\textwidth}\centering
\resizebox{\linewidth}{!}{\supportobject{0}{\only<2->{\supportmark{\kappaoneexpr}{fill=Teal}}}}
\par\scriptsize\rule{0pt}{3ex}\only<2->{Teal: points with $\kappa_1=1$, the support of $K_1$.}
\end{columns}
\source{\refLB}
\note{T_K and T_S split the selected rows into logical and stabilizer rows. The product factor forces x to equal the label at each occupied column. Second step: the first logical row of Q48 on the support. It has eight ones in each block.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}[label=sat]{SAT assigns three logical bits to each geometric point}
\talktext
Unknowns: $\kappa(\beta)\in\F_2^3$ for the 48 points, so 144 label bits.
\[
7+6s+3\binom{s}{2}=88\ \text{parity constraints at }s=6.
\]
K1--K3 give 7, KS1--KS2 give $6s$, and KS3 gives $3\binom{s}{2}$. Each is an XOR of products of up to three label bits. There are no distance constraints.
\par\medskip
\onslide<2->{A solution for $Q_{48}$ [LB26]:
\begin{center}\begin{tabular}{c|ccc}$w$ & $10$ & $01$ & $11$\\\hline
$\kappa_1(u,w)$ & $u_1\oplus u_3$ & $1\oplus u_3$ & $u_4$\end{tabular}\end{center}
$\kappa_2$ and $\kappa_3$ are quadratic in $u$ on each block. The rows must also be independent modulo $\C_2$.}
\source{\refLB}
\note{The 88 counts parity constraints, not CNF clauses. Auxiliary variables depend on the encoding. Each candidate is checked independently afterwards. Not every compatible labeling has a sign vector, so the candidates go to the next test. Full matrices are in the public repository.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}[label=signs]{The signs solve a linear system over $\mathbb Z_4$}
\talktext
\begin{columns}[c,onlytextwidth]
\column{.55\textwidth}
Write $\Gamma_j=1-2\gamma_j$ with $\gamma_j\in\{0,1\}$. Then $W_\Gamma(c)=\wt(c)-2\,\gamma\cdot c$, and every codeword $c=xK\oplus yS$ has even weight:
\[
\gamma\cdot c\equiv\tfrac12\wt(c)-2x_1x_2x_3\pmod4.
\]
\onslide<2->{For $Q_{48}$ [LB26]:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\gamma(u,w)&=u_3\oplus w_1Q_1(u)\oplus w_2Q_2(u),\\
Q_1&=u_1\oplus u_2\oplus u_3\oplus u_1u_3\oplus u_2u_3\oplus u_2u_4,\\
Q_2&=u_3\oplus u_4\oplus u_1u_2\oplus u_1u_4\oplus u_3u_4.
\end{aligned}
\]}%
\column{.42\textwidth}\centering
\resizebox{\linewidth}{!}{\supportobject{0}{\only<2->{\supportmark{\gammaexpr}{fill=Teal}\supportmark{1-\gammaexpr}{fill=Gold!45}}}}
\par\scriptsize\rule{0pt}{3ex}\only<2->{Teal: $T^\dagger$ ($6,6,10$). Gold: $T$. Total $26\,T+22\,T^\dagger$.}
\end{columns}
\takeawayat{2-}{The pattern satisfies the gate condition on all $8\cdot64=512$ codewords.}
\source{\refLB}
\note{The congruence is linear over Z4 in gamma, and gamma must be binary. Elimination over Z4 decides feasibility. The picture is the paper pattern computed from the polynomial gamma. These are coordinate positions, not a hardware layout.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}[label=witness]{A three-point witness makes the $Z$-distance exact}
\talktext
\begin{center}\resizebox{!}{20mm}{\supportobject{0}{\foreach \b in {1,2,3}{\draw[Gold,line width=1.6pt] ({5*\b},0) circle[radius=.42];}}}\end{center}
Put a $Z$ error at $u=0000$ in each block:
\[
\underbrace{10\oplus01\oplus11}_{\text{stabilizer syndrome}}=00,
\qquad
\underbrace{011\oplus110\oplus000}_{\text{label sum}}=101\ne000.
\]
The operator commutes with every check and acts nontrivially on the encoded qubits.
\takeaway{Projectivity gives $d_Z\ge3$. The witness gives $d_Z\le3$. $Q_{48}=\qcode{48}{3}{3}$ with $(d_X,d_Z)=(16,3)$.}
\note{The three columns sum to zero, so no X check sees the error. The labels at u equal to 0000 are 011, 110 and 000, and their sum is nonzero, so the error is not a stabilizer. d_X equal to 16 is computed separately.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}[label=rm-words]{Stabilizer supports are Reed--Muller words}
\talktext
For projective $S$ with support $\mathcal P\subset\F_2^s$, S1--S3 say
\[
\sum_{\beta\in\mathcal P}\beta_{i_1}\cdots\beta_{i_t}=0\pmod 2,\qquad 1\le t\le3.
\]
Following [NH22], let $c=\chi_{\mathcal P}+\epsilon\delta_0$, with $\epsilon$ chosen so that $\wt(c)$ is even. The origin changes no moment of positive degree, so $c\perp\RM(3,s)$ and
\[
c\in\RM(3,s)^\perp=\RM(s-4,s),\qquad n=\wt(c)-c(0).
\]
For $Q_{48}$: $c=w_1+w_2+w_1w_2\in\RM(2,6)$.
\takeaway{A short code needs a low-weight Reed--Muller word, then compatible labels and signs.}
\source{\refNH}
\note{Attribute the indicator method to Nezami and Haah. Their lemma places the indicator of a unital space in a Reed-Muller code. Our version uses the stabilizer-only parities. Low-weight words of Reed-Muller codes are classified, which makes the search below 39 finite after rank reduction.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}[label=floor38]{Our exclusion argument covers every length through 38}
\talktext
\begin{enumerate}\setlength{\itemsep}{6pt}
\item Reduce stabilizer rank to $s\le8$ when $n\le38$.
\item Exclude $s\le5$, then settle ranks 6 and 7 using the weight spectrum and support geometry.
\item At rank 8, reduce to finitely many normal forms and enumerate the remaining cases.
\item Show that no remaining support admits the required logical triple.
\end{enumerate}
\begin{block}{Independent result of our paper}
No CSS code with $k=3$, $d_Z\ge3$, and the nine CH conditions has $n\le38$.
\end{block}
\note{The proof combines analytic reductions with a finite enumeration. The rank-eight coverage and the logical-row exclusions are complete, unlike a sampled SAT search. The Reed-Muller classifications are cited inputs. The theorem is scoped to the nine CH conditions.}
\end{frame}

\talktext

Wills--Jain--Singh classify $n\le54$, $d_Z\ge3$, up to CNOT+$S$ output equivalence.

Their classification does not test sign feasibility for every code.

\end{frame}

\begin{frame}[label=n47]{What the 47-qubit comparison establishes}
\talktext
Jacinto et al.\ [JVB+26] give a $\qcode{47}{3}{3}$ code satisfying the nine CH conditions.
Its $\mathbb Z_4$ sign system is inconsistent [LB26], so it has no physical $T/T^\dagger$ layer, and its correction needs entangling $CZ$ gates.
\par\medskip
Wills--Jain--Singh [WJS26] establish minimum length 47 in the quasi-transversal CCZ class with $k=3$, $d_Z\ge3$. Their catalogue identifies outputs up to CNOT and $S$, not full Clifford equivalence.
\par\medskip
These facts do not establish uniqueness of the length-47 code or rule out every other length-47 completion with feasible signs.
\source{\refJ\par\refWJS\par\refLB}
\note{The infeasibility statement concerns the specified Jacinto code only. Minimum length for a physical T and T-dagger layer with no correction remains open between 47 and 48.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}[label=footprint]{Code length and distillation footprint are different resources}
\talktext
Apply the phase gates to encoded $\ket{+}^{\otimes3}$, measure checks, and decode to prepare a CCZ resource state.
\begin{itemize}
\item $n$: code length, the number of noisy $T$ inputs in the distillation description.
\item $N$: circuit footprint in the implementation of [JVB+26]. For the matrix description, $N=k+s$ [WJS26].
\item The $47$-to-$1$ distance-3 CCZ protocol has $(n,N)=(47,9)$.
\end{itemize}
At least 3 input $Z$ errors are needed for an undetected logical error in the ideal-Clifford distillation model.
A physical factory also needs a circuit, state preparation, measurements, decoding, and a noise model.
\source{\refJ\par\refWJS}
\note{Length is not the only cost. The footprint counts qubits in the distillation circuit. No resource comparison is claimed for Q48.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}[label=family]{The geometric construction produces a family}
\talktext
Choose a subspace complement
\[
\PG(p+q-1,2)\setminus\PG(p-1,2),\qquad
n=2^{p+q}-2^p=2^p(2^q-1).
\]
The paper constructs codes at 13 block lengths from 48 to 496, with $d_Z=3$ or 4.
\par\medskip
Each support needs compatible logical rows and physical signs.
Existence does not follow from the point count.
\takeaway{The next algebraic task is to characterize which supports admit the required labels and signs.}
\note{p equal to four and q equal to two gives 48. This is a family of finite constructions, not an asymptotic claim. Only the fixed-support census for 48 is complete. Other counts are lower bounds.}
\end{frame}
