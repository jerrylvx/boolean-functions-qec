\turn{a. The cube code (5 min)}
\cue{0--1.5 min / 1}{The cube code $\llbracket 8,3,2\rrbracket$}{Set the notation once: words are functions on the cube, CSS states are coset superpositions. Step 3: Z000 Z100 is a weight-two logical.}
\cue{1.5--3 min / 2}{A transversal T/T$^\dagger$ layer gives logical CCZ on the cube}{W equals the distance to p minus four. Both words of a logical pair get the same phase.}
\cue{3--4 min / 3}{Weights of sums expand into overlaps}{General formula only. Pointwise check gives signed weights too. Terms with four or more words vanish mod 8.}
\cue{4--5 min / 4}{The gate condition is a congruence on generator rows}{Coefficients 1, minus 2, 4 give the mod 8, mod 4 and mod 2 conditions. The cube satisfies them.}
\turn{b. History and the CH conditions (5 min)}
\cue{5--7 min / 5}{From magic-state distillation to triorthogonal codes}{Cite the CSS-T line of Camps-Moreno, L\'opez, Matthews et al. Nezami-Haah n+k at most 38 is not our bound.}
\cue{7--8.5 min / 6}{A triorthogonal matrix and a triorthogonal space differ}{Odd rows break the space definition. CCZ needs an odd logical triple.}
\cue{8.5--10 min / 7}{The nine CH conditions}{Mod 2 reduction of the row congruences. Quasi-transversal means up to a diagonal Clifford correction.}
\turn{c. Results and finite geometry (10 min)}
\cue{10--12 min / 8}{Result: a 48-qubit code with a transversal T/T$^\dagger$ CCZ}{State the result now that every term is defined. 39 to 46 excluded by Wills, Jain and Singh.}
\cue{12--14 min / 9}{Fix S using finite/projective geometry code}{Four steps. Only the logical rows are searched.}
\columnbreak
\cue{14--15.5 min / 10}{Projectivity rules out weight-one and weight-two Z logicals}{Parity-check argument. Covers d\_Z only.}
\cue{15.5--17.5 min / 11}{The support is projective space minus a subspace}{63 points, remove the 15 with w = 00, three blocks of 16. This picture stays for the rest of the construction.}
\cue{17.5--20 min / 12}{Hyperplane sections give the two nonzero weights}{u1 cuts each block in half: 24. w1 takes two blocks: 32. Both divisible by 8.}
\turn{d. Indicators, Reed--Muller codes, SAT (10 min)}
\cue{20--21.5 min / 13}{Column indicators record the generator matrix}{Cube: chi\_G = y. Q48: chi\_S = w1 + w2 + w1 w2.}
\cue{21.5--23.5 min / 14}{The CH conditions are inner products with monomials}{Overlap parity equals an inner product with a monomial. Coset of RM(r-4, r).}
\cue{23.5--25.5 min / 15}{The label map turns logical rows into Boolean unknowns}{chi\_G factors through the labels. Step 2 shows the support of K1.}
\cue{25.5--27 min / 16}{SAT assigns three logical bits to each geometric point}{144 label bits, 88 parity constraints, no distance constraints.}
\cue{27--28.5 min / 17}{The signs solve a linear system over $\mathbb Z_4$}{Binary unknowns, linear over Z4. Pattern 26 T plus 22 T-dagger, checked on 512 codewords.}
\cue{28.5--30 min / 18}{A three-point witness makes the Z-distance exact}{Syndromes cancel, label sum 101 is nonzero.}

\turn{e. Bounds and implications (10 min)}
\cue{30--32 min / 19}{Stabilizer supports are Reed--Muller words}{Indicator method from Nezami-Haah. Q48 gives a word of RM(2,6).}
\cue{32--34 min / 20}{Our exclusion argument covers every length through 38}{Analytic reductions plus a complete finite enumeration.}
\cue{34--36 min / 21}{Concurrent classification closes the 39--46 gap}{CNOT plus S equivalence. No uniqueness claim at 47.}
\cue{36--37.5 min / 22}{What the 47-qubit comparison establishes}{The Jacinto code has no sign vector. This concerns that code only.}
\cue{37.5--38.5 min / 23}{Code length and distillation footprint are different resources}{n is not the only cost. No resource claim for Q48.}
